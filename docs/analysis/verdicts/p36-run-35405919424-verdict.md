### 2.2 P1 — UNMEASURABLE on the graded path, but the expansions ARE recorded

**Correction to an earlier reading, recorded because the error direction matters:**

that varies the expansion is the arm that records nothing. The conclusion that
decomposition did not fix the target therefore rests on P2 (observable, and it
failed) rather than on P1. The missing trace is recorded in §5 as the prerequisite

A useful side effect: reading the expansions on the failing TR questions shows

### 2.4 P4 — FAIL

> P4: non-ABS capabilities move by at most ±1 question each.

| capability | baseline | feature | Δ questions |
|---|---|---|---|
| IE | 95.35% | 91.86% | **−3** |
| ABS | 96.55% | 96.55% | 0 |

IE moved by 3 questions, above the pre-registered ±1 threshold. Discordant pairs:

**P4's falsifier is "the expansion change leaks outside its target" and it fired.**
Decomposition perturbed single-operand retrieval in the direction of *harm*:

| IE question | outcome |
|---|---|

The mechanism is at least plausible: the added instruction tells the model to emit

single-operand questions containing those words get their expansion splintered for
no reason. This is a hypothesis about the failures, not a measured cause — the arm
does not record expansions (§2.2).

### 2.6 R4 overall

The pre-registration's own falsifier for the hypothesis in `p27` §4.1 required
decomposition to fix the target. It did not. Combined with P4 firing, R4 does not
merely fail to help — **it fails its own safety prediction**. Disposition:

- **Do not enable `decomposeConjunctions` by default.** It is not shipped anywhere; the option exists only for measurement.
- **Do not retry R4** until the expansion-channel instrument exists (§5), because a retry without it repeats the same unmeasurable experiment.
- R4's status moves from "well-founded hypothesis awaiting a run" to **"refuted as stated"**. The cost was one run at `limit=200`; the alternative was shipping an unmeasured prompt change that leaks 3 IE questions.

## 3. P32/P33: the fixes fire, the score does not move

### 3.1 The comparison

`run_35162802298` (limit=60, 379 diagnostic rows) predates both fixes;
`run35405919424` (limit=200, 171 rows) carries both. The only honest join is the
**intersection of question ids** — 171 questions — under a deterministic pipeline.

| capability | n | old acc | new acc | old✓new✗ | old✗new✓ | McNemar p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ABS | 29 | 96.6% | 96.6% | 0 | 0 | 1.000 |
| IE | 86 | 95.3% | 94.2% | 1 | 0 | 1.000 |
| KU | 28 | 78.6% | 67.9% | 3 | 0 | 0.250 |
| TR | 28 | 78.6% | 78.6% | 0 | 0 | 1.000 |
| **all** | **171** | — | — | **4** | **0** | **0.125** |

**0 gains, 4 losses on the score.** But the score is the wrong instrument for these
fixes, and §3.3 shows why: the fix changed the *content* of the answers without
changing their *correctness*, because the questions it repaired were failing on
something else too. Reporting "0 gains" alone would be true and misleading.

### 3.2 The losses are LLM-scoring noise, not code

The load-bearing test: `top1Score` is computed by the deterministic retrieval
stack, so if it is identical between runs the retrieved context is identical.

| quantity | count |
|---|---|
| questions in both runs | 171 |
| **identical `top1Score`** | **132** |
| different `top1Score` | 39 |
| **verdict flipped despite identical `top1Score`** | **4** |
| verdict flipped within the 39 score-differing questions | **0** |

**All four flips occur on questions whose retrieval signal is bit-identical.** Not
one flip is associated with a score change. Inspecting the four:

| id | old answer | new answer | `top1Score` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `6aeb4375` | `"...report the value from the latest turn: four.\n\nfour"` | `"Let me work through this chronologically."` | 0.7033 (same) |
| `b6019101` | `"5"` | `"12"` | 0.6349 (same) |
| `e493bb7c` | `"bedroom"` | `"bed"` | 0 (same) |
| `32260d93` | same text | **byte-identical text** | 0.5557 (same) |

`32260d93` is decisive: the two answers are the same string and the verdict
differs. That is the LLM judge or the answer path being non-reproducible at
`temperature=0`, not a retrieval or deterministic-engine change. `6aeb4375` shows
the same thing in a different shape — the new answer leaked a chain-of-thought
preamble (`"Let me work through this chronologically."`) that the old one did not,
with identical retrieval.

**Therefore the 4 losses are not attributable to P32/P33.** They are noise that
would have appeared without them.

### 3.3 The fixes fire — verified on their own targets

The path discriminator is the **absence of `llmRaw`**, which marks a
deterministic-engine answer: across the 171 graded rows, 24 are deterministic and
147 went through the LLM.

**P32** — TR carries 18 deterministic answers, 4 of which fail. All five
sequence/ordering questions are deterministic, i.e. they ran the fixed code:

| id | old answer | new answer | old | new |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gpt4_f49edff3` | 3 events, ordered | 3 events, ordered | ✓ | ✓ |
| `gpt4_18c2b244` | 3 events, ordered | 3 events, ordered | ✓ | ✓ |
| `gpt4_45189cb4` | 2 events, ordered | 2 events, ordered | ✗ | ✗ |
| **`gpt4_7f6b06db`** | **`"day hike to Muir Woods National Monument"`** (one event, 40 chars) | **3 events, ordered** (132 chars) | ✗ | ✗ |
| **`gpt4_7abb270c`** | **`"Natural History Museum's \"Dinosaur Fossils\" exhibition"`** (one event, 54 chars) | **6 museums, ordered** (357 chars) | ✗ | ✗ |

**The fix demonstrably repaired the reported bug.** The two questions that used to
return a single event now return a full ordered sequence — that is exactly the
`formatOrdering` recency-before-sequence defect `p32-ordering-sequence-bug.md`
described, and it is now gone in production. Two of the five were already correct
and did not regress.

They remain marked wrong because the **events themselves are wrong or incomplete**,
which is upstream of ordering:

- `gpt4_7f6b06db`: expansion is `["first trip","second trip","third trip"]` — generic, because the question does not name its events — so retrieval surfaced the wrong three turns. The correct sibling `gpt4_f49edff3` names its events and expands into them verbatim, and succeeds.
- `gpt4_45189cb4`: two events extracted where the gold names three.

So P32's observable effect is **"the bug is fixed, the question is still wrong for a
different reason"**, and the ceiling noted in the original verdict is now the
binding constraint.

**P33** — KU carries 6 deterministic answers, 3 of which fail. The single
qualifier-bearing question in the sample (`c4ea545c`, "more frequently than I did
previously") moved from `"three times a week"` to `"3 times a week"` — a
normalisation change consistent with the fix routing it differently — but is still
graded wrong. With one target question in the sample, the run has no power to
confirm or refute P33 either way; its warrant remains the unit tests.

## 5. The instrument gap this run exposed

The **R4 ablation arm emits no decision trace**, while the graded run does.
Concretely missing from `benchmark-conjunction-ablation-report.json`:

1. **No `expansionQueries` per question per arm.** P1 was unanswerable *from the R4
   artifact*; the P4 mechanism (splintering on `and`/`or`/`vs`) is inferred rather
   than shown.
2. **No baseline per-question correctness.** The artifact keeps `featureCorrect`
   only, so the 3 flipped IE questions **cannot be named from the artifact alone** —
   identifying them required rebuilding the scoped order offline. That is a
   reproducibility defect: the artifact does not contain enough to audit its own
   discordant pairs.

Before R4 is retried or any expansion change is made, the arm must emit a per-arm
per-question trace carrying the expansion queries and both verdicts. Building that
is the prerequisite, not the retry.

Note the graded path already has the right shape — `expansionQueries` and the
`retrieved` context are both in `benchmark-single-session-diagnostics.json`. The
gap is that the **ablation** path was never given the same wiring as the graded
path.

## 6. Next

1. **Do not ship `decomposeConjunctions`.** Default stays off; the option stays for measurement only.
2. **Build the ablation trace** (§5) before any further expansion work.
3. **Do not revert P32/P33.** P32 is shown repairing its bug in production; P33 is
   correct by unit test. Neither is attributable for the −4, which is noise. What
   they need is a fairer test, not a revert: a scoped arm over TR-ordering and
   KU-qualifier questions rather than the graded path, where their target
   populations are 5 and ~1.
4. **The real TR bottleneck is event identification**, not ordering. Of 18
   deterministic TR answers, 4 fail, and all three sequence failures are wrong
   *inputs* while the ordering output is correct. The tell is the expansion:
   questions that name their events expand into those events and succeed
   (`gpt4_f49edff3`); questions that describe them generically expand into
   `"first trip"`/`"second trip"` and retrieve the wrong turns (`gpt4_7f6b06db`).
   That is where TR headroom lives.
5. **KU is mostly LLM-path bound** (22 of 28 rows), so its 67.9% is governed by
   reading quality rather than deterministic code. Only 6 KU rows are deterministic,
   and 3 of those fail — a 50% rate on a small population worth a closer look.
6. **The 86.00% headline rides on ABS (28/29) and IE (81/86).** TR (78.6%) and KU
   (67.9%) are the remaining mass.
